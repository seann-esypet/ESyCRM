## The short answer

None of these tools finds salon emails *with evidence* on its own. The workable design has four steps:

1. **Turn each Place ID into the salon's official website.** Google Places, or a cheaper scraper of Google's data.
2. **Crawl that website** and record the exact page where every email and form was found.
3. **Apply rules** that drop provider, template, and directory emails and booking-only forms.
4. **Send what's left to a human review queue.**

Facebook is the weak link. The supported API route probably won't be approved for this use, and the scraper routes run against Meta's terms.

## Vendor by vendor

### Google Places — Place Details (New)

**1. Place IDs:** Yes, you pass the ID directly. No re-search needed.

**2. What it returns:** The documented fields that move a request into the Enterprise tier include phone numbers, rating, opening hours, and websiteUri. No email field appears in the field lists. So Google gives you the website, and everything else (contact page, email, source URL, Facebook link) needs your own crawler.

**3. Salon vs. provider emails, real forms vs. booking forms:** No. Google doesn't handle either distinction.

**5. Pricing:**
- Place Details Enterprise has a 1,000-event free cap, then $20.00 per 1,000 up to 100,000 events.
- The IDs Only variant of Essentials is listed as unlimited free. That makes a free first pass possible to flag dead or changed IDs.
- Google recommends refreshing place IDs older than 12 months, since they can change.

**Terms risk you should know about:** Google says you must not pre-fetch, cache, or store Places content beyond allowed exceptions, while place IDs are exempt and may be stored indefinitely. Storing the Google-supplied website URL permanently in your sheet is a gray area. The email you later find on the salon's own site is not Google content. Ask counsel if this list becomes a permanent database.

### SerpApi

**1. Place IDs:** Yes. Its Google Maps engine takes a `place_id` parameter that can be used without any other optional parameter. The docs warn that this is not the same as the place_id in its Google Search and Local APIs, which is actually the data_cid.

**2. What it returns:** Results include a `website` field and an unclaimed-listing flag, but no email. You still need a crawler.

**4. Facebook:** SerpApi has a separate Facebook Profile API. It requires a profile_id taken from the Page URL, and its output schema includes an email field for the profile. So you must already have the correct Page URL. It is scraping, not Meta's API (see the Facebook section).

**5–6. Plans and limits:**
- Starter is $25/month for 1,000 searches at 200/hour; Developer $75 for 5,000 at 1,000/hour; Production $150 for 15,000 at 3,000/hour; Big Data $275 for 30,000 at 6,000/hour. There is also a free plan with 250 searches/month at 50/hour.
- Cached, errored, and failed searches aren't counted, and refunds are available within 7 days if you've used 20% or less of your searches.
- Cached results expire after an hour and are free.

### Outscraper

**1. Place IDs:** Yes. Its Maps search query also accepts google_id, place_id, or CID.

**2. What it returns:** Emails come from a separate Emails & Contacts enrichment. It extracts up to 5 emails per website. The concern is that its product description says it pulls contacts from websites, Facebook pages, Google, DuckDuckGo, and other public sources. That mix of sources is exactly where misattributed emails come from. I could not verify that it returns a per-email source URL. Treat that as **unknown** until they confirm.

**3. Salon vs. provider emails, form types:** No documented classification.

**5. Pricing:**
- Maps: free for the first 500 businesses, then $3 per 1,000 records.
- Emails: Outscraper's own pricing explainer prices the emails scraper at $0.003 per record. Its example counts "emails," so it's unclear whether a record means one domain or one email found. Ask them.
- No monthly fees; refund within 3 days if the tools fail to retrieve data.

### Apify — Google Maps Scraper (compass/crawler-google-places)

**1. Place IDs:** Yes. The input schema has a `placeIds` array (shown in Apify's published input examples).

**2. What it returns:** Pay-per-event pricing:
- $0.007 per run start and $0.004 per place scraped.
- An optional contact-details add-on at $0.002 per place, which covers emails and social profiles from the business website, with no charge for places without websites.
- The current Store listing says from $1.50 per 1,000 places. That's a higher-plan rate, so the $0.004 figure above is the free-plan ceiling.

I could not verify that the contact add-on returns the page each email came from. Some third-party contact actors do document this. One outputs a sourceUrl and page title for each contact found. Another returns ranked emails with a source and high/medium/low confidence, priced from $4.00 per 1,000 websites.

**Apify Facebook Page actors:** Many exist, all scraping public Pages without logging in. Examples:
- One charges $2.00 per 1,000 pages and reads phone, email, and website from page metadata.
- A competing listing claims the official Apify Facebook Pages Scraper costs $12 per 1,000 pages on the free plan, plus another $13 per 1,000 for its contact-info scraper. That's a third-party claim I did not verify.

### DataForSEO

**1. Place IDs:** Yes. My Business Info accepts place_id as an identifier of the business in Google Maps.

**2. What it returns:** Profile data including contact info, but no email crawling. You need a separate crawler.

**5. Pricing:**
- Standard queue: $0.0015 per profile ($1.50 per 1,000), turnaround up to 45 minutes.
- Live mode: $0.0054 per profile, about 6 seconds on average.
- Minimum payment is $50.

**6. Limits:** Up to 2,000 API calls per minute, with at most 100 tasks per POST.

### Stronger alternative: a dedicated crawler

The real gap in every option above is evidence capture, not the Maps lookup. Use a crawler you control: Firecrawl, or a self-hosted Playwright/Crawl4AI script. Point it at the known website and save the full page for each email or form.

Firecrawl pricing:
- Hobby is $19/month for 5,000 credits, and a scrape that returns nothing isn't charged.
- Hobby, Standard and Growth credits don't roll over; every account gets 1,000 free credits a month.
- Standard is $99 monthly or $83 annual for 100,000 credits.
- Concurrency reportedly runs 5 on Hobby and 50 on Standard.

## Question 3 in practice: telling salon contacts from impostors

No vendor documents doing this reliably. Build it as rules, then human review.

**An email counts as salon-attributable only if all of these hold:**
- It was found on the salon's own domain, or on its correctly matched Facebook Page.
- Its source URL and surrounding text are captured.
- Its domain is either:
  - the website's domain, or
  - a freemail address (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.) that appears on the salon's own pages next to the salon's name.
- It is not on a blocklist of:
  - website builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy),
  - booking providers (Vagaro, GlossGenius, Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Square; for pet grooming, MoeGo, Gingr, DaySmart),
  - directories,
  - template placeholders (such as info@mysite.com).

**Before trusting any email, confirm the website belongs to the listing.** The listing's phone number or street address should appear on the site. This catches expired domains that now belong to someone else.

**A usable form must:**
- sit on the salon's domain (or be clearly embedded as the salon's contact form),
- have a free-text message field,
- not require choosing a service or time slot.

Booking widgets, newsletter signups, and "Powered by" provider forms don't count. Forms are classified from the page's HTML only and are never submitted.

## Question 4: Facebook permissions

**The supported API route.** Meta's Page Public Metadata Access requires App Review. Its allowed uses are analyzing engagement through Like and follower counts, or aggregating public About information from many Pages. Page Public Content Access is only available with business verification. My assessment: an outreach-enrichment use case is unlikely to pass review, and I couldn't verify that the Page email field is returned under either feature. Mark that **unknown**.

**The scraper route.** Meta's Automated Data Collection Terms say you may not collect data by automated means without Meta's express written permission. They add that agreeing to those terms is not itself that permission. SerpApi's Facebook Profile API and the Apify Facebook actors fall on this side.

**My recommendation:** Use Facebook only as a manual check by a reviewer, only for high-value rows with no website.

## Question 6: limits, failure modes, and evidence fields

**Rate limits and bulk handling:**
- SerpApi: per-hour throughput depends on the plan (listed above).
- DataForSEO: documented call and batch limits (listed above).
- Google: per-minute quotas are set in the Cloud Console. I didn't verify the defaults.
- Outscraper and Apify: both run asynchronous jobs with CSV/JSON export. Their throughput limits are **unknown**.

**Failure modes to expect:**
- Stale or moved Place IDs, and permanently closed businesses.
- The "website" field pointing to Facebook, Instagram, Linktree, or a booking page instead of a real site.
- Emails that are rendered by JavaScript, obfuscated ("name [at] domain"), protected by Cloudflare, or shown only as an image.
- Contact forms inside iframes (common on Wix and Squarespace).
- Chains where every location shares one corporate email.
- Parked or resold domains.
- Provider support emails sitting in site footers.

**Fields for the human review queue:**
- **Listing inputs:** place_id, name, address, phone, state.
- **Run details:** tool, run_id, retrieved_at.
- **Website match:** website as returned, final URL after redirects, and match evidence (phone/name/address found on the site, and on which page).
- **Email evidence:** email, source_url, a text snippet of about 80 characters around it, extraction method (mailto / text / deobfuscated), domain class (own / freemail / provider / unrelated), MX check.
- **Form evidence:** form_url, form type (general / booking / newsletter / quote), field list, host (own site / provider iframe).
- **Facebook:** fb_url and match evidence.
- **Review outcome:** reviewer decision, rejection reason code, review minutes.

## Pilot design

**Sample:** 240 listings, 80 per state.
- Within each state, 55% of rows have a website on Google and 45% don't. The no-website group is oversampled because that's where tools differ most.
- Deliberately include some chain locations and sites built on booking platforms.

**How to run it:**
- Run every shortlisted tool on the identical 240 Place IDs.
- A reviewer checks every contact a tool reports.
- On a 50-row "gold" subset, the reviewer also searches manually, so you can measure what the tools missed.
- Send no messages and submit no forms.

**Metrics** (all denominators are sampled listings, deduplicated by Place ID):

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Verified-email yield | Rows with ≥1 verified salon-attributable email ÷ 240 |
| Verified usable-form yield | Rows with ≥1 verified general-inquiry form ÷ 240 |
| Distinct businesses with either path | Rows with an email or a form ÷ 240 (chains also counted by domain) |
| False-positive rate | Tool-reported emails or forms rejected by the reviewer ÷ all tool-reported; tracked separately for emails and forms |
| Cost per verified contact path | Actual tool spend ÷ distinct businesses with a verified path (reviewer minutes reported alongside) |
| Time per row | Tool wall-clock time ÷ rows, with human review minutes reported separately |

None of these count as a success: a bare contact-page link, a booking link, a guessed pattern like info@domain, or any provider-owned email.

**Pilot cost is close to $0:**
- Google: 240 Enterprise calls fit inside the 1,000-event free cap.
- SerpApi: the free plan's 250 searches cover 240 lookups.
- Outscraper: the 500 free records per service cover it.
- Apify: about $1.30, inside the free credit.
- Firecrawl: its free 1,000 credits are tight, so budget $19 for Hobby.
- DataForSEO: needs the $50 minimum payment, so include it only if the others miss websites.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Best for your existing Place IDs:** Google Place Details (Enterprise field mask: `websiteUri`, `nationalPhoneNumber`, `formattedAddress`, `businessStatus`) plus a crawler you control that captures evidence. It's official data, takes your IDs directly, gives you full provenance, and costs about $266–$365 for the full run. Mind the caching terms.
2. **Cheapest all-in-one to test against it:** Apify Compass with `placeIds` and the contact add-on. Use it as the comparison arm. Adopt it only if its source evidence holds up in review.
3. **Outscraper:** Cheap, but its mix of email sources raises misattribution risk, and provenance fields are unconfirmed.
4. **DataForSEO:** The cheapest website lookup if you want to avoid Google's API. It still needs a crawler.
5. **SerpApi:** Don't upgrade for this job. It returns the same website field at the highest per-lookup cost. Its Facebook endpoint needs the Page URL you don't yet have, and it's scraping.

**What would trigger an upgrade:**
- Scale to all 13,000 when the chosen route shows a false-positive rate of 5% or less and a verified yield within 5 points of the best tool.
- Pick a cheaper tool over route 1 only if it matches route 1's yield *and* returns source URLs.
- Consider SerpApi only if it finds websites for 5% or more of the no-website rows that Google missed.
- Move to Firecrawl Standard once monthly crawling passes about 5,000 pages.

## Questions to ask each vendor before the full run

- **All vendors:** Is billing per input, per result, or per email found? Are failed or empty lookups charged? Do you return the source URL and snippet for each email? What happens to closed or moved Place IDs? What are your concurrency limits, and do unused credits expire?
- **Outscraper:** Which sources feed Emails & Contacts, and can I restrict it to the business's own domain? What exactly is a billable "record"?
- **Apify:** Does the Compass contact add-on output a source page for each email? What does my plan tier actually charge per place?
- **DataForSEO:** Is the website field always present in My Business Info results? Does the $1 trial credit work on production endpoints?
- **Google:** Can a website URL derived from Place Details be stored in a CRM, given the caching restrictions? (Ask your Maps rep or counsel.)
- **Meta:** Would an App Review for Page Public Metadata Access that aggregates About-page contact fields be approved for business outreach?

I'm not a lawyer. Have counsel review the Google caching and Meta collection terms before storing this data long-term or running automated Facebook collection.
